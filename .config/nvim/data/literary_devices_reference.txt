1. FIGURATIVE LANGUAGE & IMAGERY
================================================================================

Allegory
  Definition: A story where characters, setting, and events stand for bigger ideas or historical events.
  Example: Animal Farm by George Orwell (a mirror for the Russian Revolution).

Allusion
  Definition: A quick reference to a well-known person, event, story, or myth without explaining it.
  Example: "He had a Herculean task ahead of him."

Analogy
  Definition: A detailed comparison between two different things to make a tricky idea easier to grasp.
  Example: "Explaining a joke is like dissecting a frog. You understand it better, but the frog dies."

Anachronism
  Definition: Dropping an object, person, or custom into a time period where it doesn't belong.
  Example: A mechanical clock striking in Shakespeare's Julius Caesar.

Anthropomorphism
  Definition: Making non-human things literally act, talk, and behave like people.
  Example: Mickey Mouse wearing clothes, driving a car, and paying taxes.

Archetype
  Definition: A classic, instantly recognizable character type, symbol, or story pattern.
  Example: The reluctant hero, the wise mentor, or the grand quest.

Connotation
  Definition: The emotional baggage, vibe, or implied meaning a word carries beyond its dictionary definition.
  Example: "Home" feels warm and safe, while "house" just sounds like a building.

Denotation
  Definition: The literal, plain dictionary definition of a word.
  Example: "Snake" - a legless reptile.

Euphemism
  Definition: A polite or soft phrase used to avoid saying something harsh, sad, or awkward.
  Example: "Passed away" instead of "died," or "letting someone go" instead of "firing them."

Dysphemism
  Definition: An intentionally crude or blunt phrase used instead of a normal one.
  Example: "Snail mail" for regular post, or "worm food" for a dead body.

Hyperbole
  Definition: Wild exaggeration used to make a point or be funny.
  Example: "I've told you a million times!"

Imagery
  Definition: Sensory details that help the reader see, hear, smell, taste, or feel what's happening.
  Example: "The crisp autumn air smelled of burnt leaves and damp earth."

Idiom
  Definition: An established phrase that makes no sense if you translate the words literally.
  Example: "Bite the bullet" or "break a leg."

Juxtaposition
  Definition: Putting two totally different things side by side to highlight their contrast.
  Example: "It was the best of times, it was the worst of times."

Metaphor
  Definition: Stating that one thing *is* another thing to show a shared quality (no "like" or "as").
  Example: "Time is a thief."

Metonymy
  Definition: Referring to something by using the name of something closely linked to it.
  Example: "The White House" for the US President, or "Wall Street" for big finance.

Oxymoron
  Definition: A two-word phrase made of contradictory terms.
  Example: "Deafening silence," "bittersweet," or "living dead."

Paradox
  Definition: A statement that sounds self-contradictory at first, but actually makes sense when you think about it.
  Example: "I can resist everything except temptation." — Oscar Wilde

Personification
  Definition: Giving human actions or traits to objects or nature.
  Example: "The wind whispered through the trees."

Simile
  Definition: Comparing two different things using "like" or "as."
  Example: "He was as quiet as a mouse."

Symbolism
  Definition: Using a physical object, color, or action to represent an abstract idea.
  Example: A red rose for love, or a dove for peace.

Synecdoche
  Definition: Using a part of something to refer to the whole thing (or vice versa).
  Example: "All hands on deck" (hands = people), or "nice wheels" (wheels = car).

Synesthesia
  Definition: Mixing up sensory descriptions, like describing a sound with a color or a sight with a touch.
  Example: "A loud yellow shirt," or "a sweet voice."

2. RHETORICAL & PERSUASIVE DEVICES
================================================================================

Anaphora
  Definition: Repeating the same word or phrase at the start of back-to-back clauses or sentences.
  Example: "We shall fight on the beaches, we shall fight on the landing grounds, we shall fight in the fields..."

Antimetabole
  Definition: Flipping the order of words in two matching clauses (A-B, B-A).
  Example: "Ask not what your country can do for you — ask what you can do for your country."

Antithesis
  Definition: Pairing direct opposites together in balanced sentence structures.
  Example: "To err is human; to forgive, divine."

Apostrophe
  Definition: When a speaker addresses someone absent, a dead person, an object, or an abstract idea directly.
  Example: "O Death, where is thy sting?"

Asyndeton
  Definition: Cutting out conjunctions (and, but, or) between phrases to speed up the pace.
  Example: "I came, I saw, I conquered."

Polysyndeton
  Definition: Cramming extra conjunctions between every item in a list for emphasis or weight.
  Example: "We have ships and men and money and stores."

Chiasmus
  Definition: Reversing sentence structure in consecutive phrases so the grammar mirrors itself.
  Example: "Despised by many, and by many praised."

Ethos
  Definition: Building trust by pointing to authority, credentials, or character.
  Example: "As a doctor with 20 years of practice, I recommend this treatment."

Logos
  Definition: Using hard facts, numbers, and clear logic to back up an argument.
  Example: "Studies show seatbelts reduce the risk of serious injury by 45%."

Pathos
  Definition: Tugging at the audience's heartstrings or triggering emotional responses to convince them.
  Example: "If we don't adopt this puppy today, he spends another cold night alone in a cage."

Epistrophe
  Definition: Repeating the exact same word or phrase at the end of back-to-back sentences.
  Example: "Government of the people, by the people, for the people."

Hypophora
  Definition: Asking a question and immediately answering it yourself.
  Example: "Why is training essential? Because practice creates perfection."

Litotes
  Definition: Making a point by denying its opposite (usually a double negative).
  Example: "She's not bad looking" (meaning she looks good).

Parallelism
  Definition: Matching sentence structures or phrasing to create rhythm and balance.
  Example: "Easy come, easy go."

Rhetorical Question
  Definition: Asking something to make people think, not because you're expecting an answer.
  Example: "Do you want to live forever?"

Understatement
  Definition: Playing something down so it sounds way smaller or less severe than it actually is.
  Example: "It's a bit chilly today" (standing in a blizzard).

3. SOUND & POETIC DEVICES
================================================================================

Alliteration
  Definition: Repeating the same starting consonant sound across nearby words.
  Example: "Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers."

Assonance
  Definition: Repeating vowel sounds inside neighboring words without matching the consonants.
  Example: "The rain in Spain stays mainly in the plain."

Consonance
  Definition: Repeating consonant sounds near or at the end of words close together.
  Example: "The lumpy, bumpy road."

Cacophony
  Definition: Using harsh, clashing, or noisy sounds on purpose to make reading feel rough.
  Example: "Kicking, cracking, clack of sticks and rocks."

Euphony
  Definition: Choosing smooth, soft, melodic sounds that roll off the tongue.
  Example: "Cellar door," "slumber," "silken, soft, serene."

Onomatopoeia
  Definition: Words that spell out the exact sound they make.
  Example: "Buzz," "hiss," "clang," "pop," "sizzle."

Repetition
  Definition: Using a word, line, or phrase multiple times to hammer home an idea or build rhythm.
  Example: "Never, never, never give up."

Rhyme
  Definition: Matching sounds at the ends of lines, inside lines, or near-matches.
  Example: Cat / Hat (end rhyme); "Once upon a midnight dreary, while I pondered, weak and weary" (internal).

Rhythm & Meter
  Definition: The underlying beat of stressed and unstressed syllables in a line of verse.
  Example: "To BE or NOT to BE, THAT is the QUES-tion."

4. NARRATIVE STRUCTURE & PLOT DEVICES
================================================================================

Chekhov's Gun
  Definition: The rule that if you point out a detail early on, it needs to pay off later in the story.
  Example: If a shotgun hangs above the fireplace in Act 1, someone better shoot it by Act 3.

Cliffhanger
  Definition: Ending a scene or chapter right at the climax of tension so the reader can't put the book down.
  Example: A character slipping off a ledge right as the chapter ends.

Deus Ex Machina
  Definition: A lazy or sudden fix that magically solves an unwinnable problem out of nowhere.
  Example: An unmentioned army randomly arriving to win the battle at the last second.

Epiphany
  Definition: A sudden "aha!" moment when a character finally gets the truth about themselves or the plot.
  Example: Realizing the person helping you was the villain all along.

Flashback
  Definition: Cutting away from the current timeline to show something that happened in the past.
  Example: Pausing a fight scene to show the childhood event that started the feud.

Foreshadowing
  Definition: Leaving small clues early on about what's going to go down later.
  Example: A stray comment about bad brakes right before a road trip.

Frame Narrative
  Definition: A main story that acts as the shell for another story told inside it.
  Example: Frankenstein (letters from a sea captain host Victor's tale).

In Media Res
  Definition: Dropping the reader straight into the middle of the action instead of setting things up slowly.
  Example: Opening the book mid-car chase.

MacGuffin
  Definition: An object or goal that drives the entire plot forward, even if the object itself isn't what matters.
  Example: The briefcase in Pulp Fiction or the ring in Lord of the Rings.

Plot Twist
  Definition: A sudden pivot in the plot that flips what the audience thought was happening on its head.
  Example: Learning the narrator was dead the whole time.

Red Herring
  Definition: A fake clue or suspicious character meant to throw readers off the real track.
  Example: A shady butler who looks guilty but had nothing to do with the murder.

Ticking Clock
  Definition: A hard deadline hanging over the characters that amps up the pressure.
  Example: A bomb timer set for ten minutes.

5. CHARACTERIZATION & PERSPECTIVE
================================================================================

Antagonist
  Definition: The main character, system, or force working against the protagonist.
  Example: Darth Vader in Star Wars.

Anti-Hero
  Definition: A lead character who lacks traditional heroic virtues like honesty, selflessness, or noble motives.
  Example: Walter White, Deadpool, or Jay Gatsby.

Dynamic Character
  Definition: A character who changes, grows, or turns worse over the course of the narrative.
  Example: Ebenezer Scrooge in A Christmas Carol.

Static Character
  Definition: A character who stays pretty much the exact same from start to finish.
  Example: Sherlock Holmes.

Foil
  Definition: A character whose personality directly contrasts another's to make their traits stand out more.
  Example: Dr. Watson's plain pragmatism vs. Sherlock Holmes' erratic genius.

First-Person POV
  Definition: Telling the story directly through the eyes of a character using "I" or "we."
  Example: "I walked down the dark street."

Second-Person POV
  Definition: Writing directly to the reader using "you."
  Example: "You walk into a dark room and see a door."

Third-Person Limited POV
  Definition: Telling the story through "he," "she," or "they," but stuck inside one character's head at a time.

Third-Person Omniscient POV
  Definition: An all-knowing narrator who can jump into any character's mind and see everything happening.

Unreliable Narrator
  Definition: A narrator you can't fully trust because they're lying, confused, biased, or insane.
  Example: The narrator in Poe's The Tell-Tale Heart.

Protagonist
  Definition: The main character driving the story forward.
  Example: Elizabeth Bennet in Pride and Prejudice.

6. WORDPLAY, HUMOR & IRONY
================================================================================

Dramatic Irony
  Definition: When the audience knows something crucial that the characters on page or stage don't know yet.
  Example: We know Juliet is just asleep from a potion, but Romeo thinks she's actually dead.

Situational Irony
  Definition: When what actually happens is the complete opposite of what was expected.
  Example: A fire station burning to the ground.

Verbal Irony
  Definition: Saying one thing when you mean the exact opposite (sarcasm fits here).
  Example: Looking out at a storm and saying, "Lovely weather today."

Malapropism
  Definition: Mixing up two words that sound similar with hilarious results.
  Example: "He is the very pineapple of politeness" (meaning pinnacle).

Parody
  Definition: Spoofing a specific work or genre by exaggerating its tropes for laughs.
  Example: Don Quixote poking fun at knight romances.

Pun
  Definition: Playing on different meanings of a word or words that sound the same for humor.
  Example: "I used to be a banker, but I lost interest."

Satire
  Definition: Using humor, exaggeration, or irony to mock real-world stupidity, politics, or social flaws.
  Example: Jonathan Swift's A Modest Proposal.


================================================================================
7. DRAMATIC & STAGE TECHNIQUES
================================================================================

Aside
  Definition: A quick line spoken directly to the audience that the other characters on stage can't hear.
  Example: Characters turning to the crowd in Shakespeare plays.

Catharsis
  Definition: The heavy emotional release or relief the audience feels at the end of a intense drama.
  Example: The mix of sadness and relief when Hamlet ends.

Hamartia
  Definition: The fatal flaw or terrible mistake that triggers a tragic hero's downfall.
  Example: Excessive pride (hubris) in Oedipus Rex.

Hubris
  Definition: Blind, dangerous overconfidence or pride that usually ends in disaster.
  Example: Icarus flying too close to the sun.

Monologue
  Definition: A long speech by one character delivered to other characters present in the scene.

Soliloquy
  Definition: A character talking out loud to themselves on stage, letting the audience hear their raw thoughts.
  Example: Hamlet's "To be or not to be" speech.


================================================================================
8. STYLE, SYNTAX & DICTION
================================================================================

Archaism
  Definition: Intentionally using old-fashioned or outdated words to sound old-school.
  Example: Throwing in "thou," "thee," or "wherefore."

Colloquialism
  Definition: Slang, informal words, or everyday speech patterns used instead of formal language.
  Example: "Gonna," "y'all," or "out of the blue."

Diction
  Definition: The specific word choices an author makes to set a tone or style.
  Example: Choosing raw, street-level words versus formal, academic vocabulary.

Inversion
  Definition: Flipping standard word order around to make a line fit a rhythm or sound punchier.
  Example: "Glowed the fire bright" instead of "The fire glowed bright."

Tone
  Definition: The writer's overall attitude toward the subject matter.
  Example: Bitter, playful, serious, or cynical.

Mood
  Definition: The vibe or emotional atmosphere a scene creates for the reader.
  Example: Spooky, tense, upbeat, or dreary.
